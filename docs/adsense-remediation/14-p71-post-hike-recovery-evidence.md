## Official evidence used

- CDC/NIOSH, rhabdomyolysis signs and symptoms: severe-than-expected muscle pain, weakness/fatigue, and tea- or cola-coloured urine require prompt medical evaluation; symptoms alone cannot confirm the condition.
  <https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/rhabdo/signs-symptoms/index.html>
- U.S. NIAMS, sports injury diagnosis and recovery: persistent pain and significant injury require professional assessment, with gradual rehabilitation and return.
  <https://www.niams.nih.gov/health-topics/sports-injuries/diagnosis-treatment-and-steps-to-take>
- U.S. HHS Physical Activity Guidelines: injury risk increases with a large gap from usual activity and activity should increase gradually.
  <https://health.gov/paguidelines/second-edition/pdf/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf>
- Australian Institute of Sport recovery resources: recovery nutrition, hydration, water immersion, sleep, and training decisions are contextual support areas rather than one universal hiking timetable.
  <https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/rest-hub/recovery>
